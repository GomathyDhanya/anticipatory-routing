What is real, and what is simulated?

Real: OpenStreetMap road geometry and OSRM driving routes between geocoded city centers. Context roads provide orientation; only the highlighted corridor network is simulated. Routes via Tasman and Montague use explicit intermediate waypoints.

Simulated: All vehicles, departures, capacity restrictions, queues, colors, and trip times. Capacity stress is a hypothetical reduction on one CA 237 segment, not a reported incident. Dots follow recorded simulator events; they are not tracked cars or a live traffic feed.

Comparison: Identical vehicles, departure times, and road capacities in both panels. Five-sixths of vehicles are controlled; the rest follow static routes. Predictive, anticipatory, and cooperative baselines know background departures within a five-minute forecast horizon. RL uses current vehicle positions and route commitments. Queues have unlimited storage and do not physically spill back. Vehicles wait at road entry points; waiting markers are spread slightly for visibility.

Limits: Route alternatives are limited to six paths on the union of three sourced corridors. This is not a citywide routing graph. Turn restrictions at newly recombined junctions are not modeled. Base traversal times come from cached OSRM estimates rounded to ten-second ticks, not current observed speeds. Demo capacities are assumptions, not measured lane capacity. Replay interpolates motion between discrete simulator events.
